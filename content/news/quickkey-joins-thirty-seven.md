# QuickKey Joins Thirty Seven

Thirty Seven acquired the assets behind QuickKey in September 2026. We are continuing the product for a straightforward reason: paper assessments are still useful, and teachers should not have to spend their evening turning a stack of answer sheets into something they can act on.

QuickKey is an established classroom tool, not a new product we are introducing from scratch. Our role is to preserve what teachers find useful and keep improving the software around it.

## Keep the useful part

The basic idea remains simple. A teacher creates an answer key, students complete a supported QuickKey paper form, and the teacher scans the sheets with a phone. Reviewing responses by student and question helps turn marking into a decision about what needs another explanation.

Students do not need a device for that paper workflow. Teachers can keep their own questions rather than moving every assessment into a new format. QuickKey also has a separate online quiz workflow for classrooms using student devices.

That combination is worth preserving: familiar paper assessments, with less repetitive marking and more useful results.

## What has changed already

The acquisition was in September. The rebuilt mobile apps followed later: iOS 1.0.0 was released on November 3, 2026, and Android 1.0.0 is in a controlled production rollout.

This first native release brings a rebuilt interface for iOS and Android, a paper-scanning workflow with explicit review of captured answers, and clearer student and question results. The apps support English and Spanish. Returning teachers can sign in with their existing account; the iOS release asks them to sign in again after updating.

We have also refreshed the QuickKey website, added a Spanish homepage and signup handoff, and improved account recovery and account and payment emails. These quieter changes help teachers get back into an account and understand their plan.

The artwork on this page comes from the published native-app store listing and uses fictional classroom examples.

## What comes next

Our first priorities are keeping QuickKey stable, monitoring how it performs, and modernizing the mobile apps and web app.

We will keep listening to teachers, fixing the problems that interrupt their work, and improving the product in measured steps.
